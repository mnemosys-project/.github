SETTING
Night on the slope of Mount Helicon under a breaking storm. The location is at
once an ancient Greek sanctuary and a concert stage — the same structure
serving both purposes, not one decorating the other. Ruined temples,
colonnades and broken terraces climb the mountainside into the dark behind the
stage. Weathered stone columns frame the extreme left and right edges of the
image.

REAR CENTRE — MNEME
A colossal bronze cult statue of a seated woman wearing a laurel crown, deeply
oxidized to green verdigris, scaled so large she reads as architecture rather

continuously from her cupped hands, falls in a bright cascade down the rock

clouds backlights her with shafts of pale light.
Her name is carved in weathered Roman capitals into the rock directly beneath

THE STAGE
A long stage built from a wall of stacked amplifier cabinets, their grille

roots.

STAGE LEFT — MELETE

MELETE

Her name is carved in Roman capitals into the front face of her plinth:
AOEDE

THE TWO WOMEN ARE ALIVE — THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT THING IN THE IMAGE
Melete and Aoede must be unmistakably living human beings, not statues and not

They must also read as equals: identical size, identical height, symmetrically
balanced to left and right of the colossus, and both large and prominent in

COSTUME — RESTRAINED AND PRACTICAL
Both women wear a full-length Doric chiton of heavy, opaque woven linen in

The cloth is thick and matte. Even soaked through it stays opaque and hangs in

They are working musicians dressed for a long night's labour in bad weather,
