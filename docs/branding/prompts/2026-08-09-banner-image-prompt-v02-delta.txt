Keep the previous image exactly as it is. Same composition, same camera
position, same figures in the same places, same lighting and storm, same
colour palette, same architecture, same instruments, same text and the same
spelling of every word. Change only the five things listed below, and change
nothing else.

1. MELETE AND AOEDE ARE LIVING WOMEN, NOT STATUES.
   The two women on the plinths currently look like pale stone or marble
   figures. They must instead be unmistakably alive: warm living skin with
   real colour in the face, throat, arms and hands, flushed with effort, damp
   hair, sweat catching the amber light. They are working musicians standing
   in the rain, not sculpture.
   The colossal seated figure at the rear stays exactly as she is — weathered
   green bronze. She is the only statue among the three, and the contrast
   between her metal and their living skin should be immediately obvious.

2. MAKE THE BRONZE AUTOMATA CLEARLY VISIBLE.
   The small bronze attendant figures at the left and right wings are too dark
   and too small to read. Bring them forward onto the stage and enlarge them
   so each is clearly legible — roughly waist-height relative to the two
   women. Light them so their polished bronze catches the warm amber glow from
   the amplifiers.
   They remain plainly attendants and nothing more: one coiling a cable, one
   holding a sheet of music up to the light, one adjusting a microphone stand.
   None of them touches an instrument, stands on a plinth, or resembles either
   of the women.

3. ADD MELETE'S WAX TABLET.
   On the amplifier cabinet beside the left-hand woman, next to the bronze
   pyramid metronome, add a shallow wooden writing tablet filled with dark
   wax, its surface scratched with rows of musical notation, with a bright
   polished bronze stylus lying across it.

4. APPLY THE MATERIAL RULE SO IT IS VISIBLE.
   The rule is: bronze is bright where it is handled every day, and green
   where it is neglected.
   Oxidize the instruments that stand untouched on their stands — the kithara,
   the electric bass, the Flying V guitar, the hurdy-gurdy and the aulos
   should all show green verdigris patina across their metal parts.
   Keep bright polished bronze only where hands touch metal daily: the
   metronome, the stylus, the automata's hands, the fretboards worn clean by
   fingers, and the rim of the spring basin.

5. CHANGE THE LEFT-HAND WOMAN'S EXPRESSION.
   She currently looks startled. She should look absorbed and effortful
   instead — brow furrowed in concentration, eyes down on the sheet in front
   of her, lips parted as she counts a beat under her breath.

Do not alter the sign, the carved names, the blackletter lettering, the
Pegasus relief, the amplifier wall, the waterfall, the ruins on the
mountainside, the rain, or the framing. Do not add any new text.
