────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Create a single ultra-wide cinematic banner image, aspect ratio 2.5:1.

STYLE AND LIGHT
Photorealistic, richly detailed cinematic render. Lit like a Gustave Doré
engraving translated into film: deep storm chiaroscuro, one dramatic backlight
source breaking through cloud behind the central figure, heavy rain-charged
atmosphere with visible falling rain. Serious and monumental, never whimsical.

SETTING
Night on the slope of Mount Helicon under a breaking storm. The location is at
once an ancient Greek sanctuary and a concert stage — the same structure
serving both purposes, not one decorating the other. Ruined temples,
colonnades and broken terraces climb the mountainside into the dark behind the
stage. Weathered stone columns frame the extreme left and right edges of the
image.

REAR CENTRE — MNEME
A colossal bronze cult statue of a seated woman, wearing a laurel crown,
deeply oxidized to green verdigris, scaled so large she reads as architecture
rather than as a person. She holds no instrument and is not performing. Water
pours continuously from her cupped hands, falls in a bright cascade down the
rock beneath her, and runs into a stone basin at stage level. A break in the
storm clouds backlights her with shafts of pale light.
Her name is carved in weathered Roman capitals into the rock plinth directly
beneath her: MNEME

THE STAGE
A long stage built from a wall of stacked amplifier cabinets, their grille
cloth reading as weathered stone, their corners shod in bronze, stacked like
ashlar masonry. Warm amber pilot lights glow along the amplifier panels. A
winged horse — Pegasus — is carved in high relief into the centre face of the
cabinet wall. Thick cables run across the wet stone stage floor like vines and
roots.

STAGE LEFT — MELETE
A living young woman standing on a stone plinth, caught mid-practice and in
motion. She wears a chiton with a broad studded leather belt and a laurel
wreath. She is working, not performing: brow furrowed in concentration, eyes
down, lips parted as she counts a beat under her breath.
In front of her a music stand holds a printed sheet showing both five-line
staff notation and six-line tablature. On the amplifier cabinet beside her sit
a mechanical pyramid metronome in bright polished bronze, and a shallow wooden
writing tablet filled with dark wax, its surface scratched with rows of
notation, a bright bronze stylus lying across it.
Her name is carved in Roman capitals into the face of her plinth: MELETE

STAGE RIGHT — AOEDE
A living young woman mirrored to Melete at exactly the same scale, the same
eye level, and the same prominence, on an identical stone plinth. She is
singing — head back, mouth open, mid-note. She holds nothing; one hand is
raised above her in the mano cornuta, index and little fingers extended.
Behind her stand tall racks of rolled scrolls and handwritten set lists, and a
second music stand.
Her name is carved in Roman capitals into the face of her plinth: AOEDE

THE TWO WOMEN ARE ALIVE — THIS IS IMPORTANT
Melete and Aoede must be unmistakably living human beings, not statues and not
marble: warm skin with real colour in the face, throat and hands, flushed with
effort, hair damp from the rain, sweat catching the amber light. The colossal
seated figure behind them is the only statue in the image, and the contrast
between her weathered metal and their living skin should be immediate and
obvious.
They must also read as equals: identical size, identical height, symmetrically
balanced to left and right of the colossus.

INSTRUMENTS
Standing on stands across the stage, spanning 2,500 years, with no single
instrument favoured and none held by anyone: an ancient Greek kithara, an
aulos (double reed pipe), a hurdy-gurdy, a Flying V electric guitar, an
electric bass, a theremin, and a drum kit. Place the kithara and the Flying V
side by side so their silhouettes echo one another.

THE WINGS — BRONZE AUTOMATA
Bronze automata attend the rig at both wings, clearly visible and standing
forward on the stage where the amber amplifier light catches their polished
metal. Each is roughly waist-height relative to the two women. One coils a
cable, one holds a sheet of music up to the light, one adjusts a microphone
stand. They are plainly attendants and servants: none stands on a plinth, none
touches an instrument, and none resembles either woman.

MATERIAL RULE — APPLY THIS VISIBLY
Bronze is bright where it is handled every day, and green where it is
neglected.
Bright polished bronze: the metronome, the stylus, the automata's hands, the
guitar and bass fretboards worn clean by fingers, the rim of the spring basin.
Deep green verdigris patina: the colossal statue, the architecture, the
amplifier fittings, the drum kit shells, and the metal parts of every
instrument standing untouched on its stand.

TEXT (render cleanly, spell exactly)
- MNEME — Roman capitals carved into the rock plinth beneath the colossus
- MELETE — Roman capitals carved into the left-hand plinth
- AOEDE — Roman capitals carved into the right-hand plinth
- MNEMOSYS — heavy blackletter across the centre of the amplifier wall,
  positioned like a band logo
- On a weathered wooden signboard hanging from a wrought-iron bracket by
  chains at the upper right of the frame, two lines:
      PRACTICE BECOMES MEMORY
      MEMORY BECOMES SONG

COLOUR
Dominated by oxidized copper green and deep teal shadow. The only warm light
is amber ember glow from the valve amplifiers and the pale storm break behind
the colossus. Living skin, bone and weathered stone provide the highlights.

COMPOSITION
Keep every figure and all text within the central horizontal band of the
frame.

DO NOT INCLUDE
No instrument held in anyone's hands. No science-fiction or humanoid robots.
No skulls, pentagrams, inverted crosses, or fire. No modern LED lighting rig,
truss, or lasers. No audience or crowd. No text other than that specified
above.
